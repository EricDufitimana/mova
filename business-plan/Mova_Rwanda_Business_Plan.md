# Mova: dependable local moto pickup
A Rwanda-specific business model for an ASYV–Ntunga pilot | 10 November 2026

## Executive decision

Fund a small validation pilot, not a national rollout. The transport-access problem is credible; local demand, willingness to pay, permitted pricing and paid partnerships remain unproven.

Mova helps adults around ASYV and Ntunga request a licensed local moto, identify the correct plate, communicate pickup details and pay the driver after the trip. We start with one corridor, a small vetted driver roster and assisted booking through partner desks. We aim to earn an approved completed-trip service fee and institutional coordination subscriptions. We will expand only when pickup reliability, driver take-home earnings, repeat usage and fee collection meet measured targets.

Proposed maximum cash funding: RWF 2,850,000 for 90 days. The zero-revenue spending ceiling is RWF 2,844,640, including 15% contingency. Release only RWF 150,000 for discovery first, within the setup budget. Do not buy motorcycles.

The month-three planning case produces a RWF 84,800 cash operating shortfall; it is not profitable. Base cash break-even needs 2,059 completed app trips/month, about 79.2/day or 3.96/driver/day, with two paying institutional desks.

Status: secondary research plus a working presentation prototype. No primary interviews, verified local demand, signed driver agreements, paid institutional contracts or regulatory approvals are claimed. All forecasts are assumptions, not promises.

## Spend in stages: classroom, discovery, then conditional operation

Classroom presentation only: an optional RWF 25,000 materials/data cap (printing 15,000, markers/materials 5,000, backup data 5,000), reusing the existing demo, phones and projector. These are estimates, not school charges. This amount is part of the RWF 50,000 setup materials allowance if the business pilot proceeds, not an additional funding request.

Conditional operation: release the remaining pilot funding only after evidence of local pain, a licensed operating partner, required permissions and manageable insurance/data requirements. If these conditions cannot be met affordably, present the research and prototype and stop before operating real trips.

## 1. Does the problem matter?

Specific job: an adult at a dispersed institution, home or workplace needs a dependable connection to the Ntunga moto rank/main road, without walking to find a driver or repeatedly calling uncertain contacts.

Hypotheses to measure: wasted pickup-search time, unclear meeting points, avoidable no-shows and poor trip accountability. These are not established local statistics. The user's preference for discussing an offer is an idea to test, not proof that all Rwandans prefer bargaining.

The national access evidence and ASYV's published directions justify investigating the corridor. They do not establish how many local adults will use Mova, how often existing motos fail, or willingness to pay.

A map app cannot create road capacity or make an unavailable driver appear. The operational product is a vetted roster, honest availability, clear pickup communication and supported completion. The animated prototype is not evidence of actual pickup times or demand.

Do not lead with 'Rwanda has no moto apps', 'cashless payment is new', or 'we eliminate accidents'. Existing providers already offer overlapping features. The proposed distinction is dependable, locally supported pickup on a tightly defined corridor.

## 2. Positioning and alternatives

Proposed positioning: a dependable local pickup service with digital coordination and human backup. Negotiation, maps and payment records are features, not a defensible business on their own.

The initial service area is a deliberately small ASYV–Ntunga catchment, with consenting adults. One hundred active customers and 20 licensed drivers are targets to test, not existing users. At the base volume, 100 active adults would need 15.6 completed trips each per month. At break-even that rises to about 20.6.

Before setting an acquisition target, count actual staff/visitor/local-worker journeys in the permitted catchment. Do not use national population, all ASYV residents or all Rwamagana residents as paying users.

| Alternative | Existing advantage | Mova must prove |
|---|---|---|
| Walk to rank / street hail | No app installation or commission; familiar habit. | Mova must save meaningful search/pickup effort. |
| Call / WhatsApp a known driver | Trust and direct discussion at low coordination cost. | Roster availability, fallback drivers and accountable records must be better. |
| YEGOMOTO [S5] | Provider describes metering, cash/cashless payment, trip IDs and complaints. | Do not claim these are unique. Check current Ntunga coverage and fees in the field; the FAQ has legacy wording. |
| Other mobility providers | Competitors and new entrants may already serve the wider area. | Interview locally and request actual journeys before claiming a coverage gap. No current market-share numbers are asserted. |

## 3.1 Canvas: Key Partners

Contract with a licensed local cooperative or authorized operator; check each driver's permit, plate, insurance and passenger helmet. Do not buy motorcycles or employ unlicensed riders to make the pilot look active.

Seek permission from ASYV and other nearby workplaces/guesthouses for adult staff/visitor booking desks. They are prospective partners, not signed customers. Use school branding or grounds only with permission.

Consult RURA on platform licensing, fare rules and the proposed fee, and DPO on registration, location processing and hosting. Seek a qualified legal/accounting review.

Use a compliant hosting supplier and Google Maps where authorized. Start with rider-to-driver cash/MoMo after the ride; a future licensed payment partner would handle collection and reconciliation rather than Mova holding rider deposits.

## 3.2 Canvas: Key Activities

Recruit a maximum of 20 pilot drivers; verify documents, train on accurate online status and the pickup-code sequence, and define daytime service windows and escalation responsibilities.

Offer Kinyarwanda-first onboarding with English available. Assist customers without smartphones through an authorized desk or phone contact; do not build an expensive USSD service before demand is proven.

Maintain HTTPS, real authentication, participant-scoped location access, monitoring and backups. Replace simulated driver movement with consented real updates before claiming live commercial tracking.

Reconcile only completed trips weekly with the cooperative, resolve errors transparently, and measure request-to-pickup time, completion, no-shows, incidents, repeat use, driver earnings and cash collection.

## 3.3 Canvas: Key Resources

The existing two-device prototype demonstrates the concept. It is not yet a verified-driver commercial service, payment system or insurance guarantee.

Two borrowed test phones, founder laptops, consented driver data, Kinyarwanda support materials and a documented service process keep capital needs low.

One part-time coordinator covers defined pilot windows. Founders and a mentor provide development and escalation time in kind; this is a real resource with an explicit replacement cost.

A ring-fenced RWF 2.85 million pilot envelope funds a limited experiment. Any extra permit or insurance requirement beyond reserves must be funded or the launch reduced/paused.

## 3.4 Canvas: Value Propositions

Passengers: a known plate, clear meeting point, honest driver status, communication and a post-trip payment sequence. A pickup code adds accountability; it is not a guarantee of physical safety.

Drivers: additional local requests and less avoidable search/empty travel, provided measured take-home income improves after all fees, fuel and data costs. No guaranteed earnings claim.

Institutions: a supported driver roster, an assisted booking route, a trip log and defined issue handling. Test whether these replace enough staff coordination work to justify a subscription.

Respect current fare and meter rules. Retain negotiated offers only where the applicable regulator permits them; otherwise show the lawful tariff/quote and retain pickup communication and choice of driver.

## 3.5 Canvas: Customer Relationships

Onboard drivers face to face and show customers the complete service using the existing prototype. Ask about their current solution before promoting a new one.

Publish support hours and service boundaries. Log no-shows and complaints, suspend unsafe/unverified drivers through a documented process, and never imply a report summons emergency services.

Let adults repeat a preferred driver relationship, while keeping a dispatcher/roster useful when that driver is unavailable. Convenience and accountable records must earn repeat use; do not assume customers stay to pay a commission.

Discuss weekly earnings and collection with drivers and monthly results with each institutional buyer. Do not sell precise location histories or personal data as a revenue stream.

## 3.6 Canvas: Customer Segments

Start with adult staff, approved visitors, nearby workers and residents who actually make corridor journeys. Permission from the institution and local operator is required; this plan assumes no existing agreement.

Target 100 active adult customers as a testable acquisition goal, not an existing audience. At 15.6 completed rides/customer/month they would support the month-three volume assumption.

Drivers are supply partners and proposed fee payers. Institutional desk managers/budget holders are the second buyer group. Passengers pay the lawful transport fare to the driver and are not charged a separate assumed platform surcharge.

Exclude unaccompanied children and student transport from the first pilot. Do not present ASYV's entire population or Rwanda's population as reachable paying customers.

## 3.7 Canvas: Channels

Use the responsive web app rather than paying for separate native Android/iOS launches. Show route/status and plate selection but avoid repeated unnecessary map loads.

Acquire drivers through cooperative/rank meetings and passengers through permitted institutional demonstrations, posters and referrals. Broad paid social-media campaigns are outside the pilot budget.

Provide assisted booking by a permitted staff desk or a pilot phone contact for adults without smartphones. Budget limited support hours; do not promise a 24/7 call centre.

Sell the institutional service directly to a named budget holder using a short paid trial, agreed scope and renewal review. The institutional administration/reporting workflow can initially be manual; it is not already a finished app feature.

## 3.8 Canvas: Cost Structure

Keep the model asset-light: no motorcycle purchases, office lease, fleet fuel, large launch adverts or proprietary payment wallet. Existing vehicles/insurance remain the operator's responsibility, subject to verification.

Base cash operating budget is RWF 460,000/month plus RWF 2,000 data contribution for each of 20 drivers. Include actual human support and compliance work; a cheap website alone is not an operating business.

The RWF 20 per completed ride is a planning provision for incremental messaging/reconciliation and ordinary service errors, not a quoted payment-processing tariff or accident insurance.

Founder development/support time, borrowed devices and space are disclosed in-kind. Income tax, quoted extra permits/insurance, full-time salaries and a public launch are not silently covered by the pilot figures.

## 3.9 Canvas: Revenue Streams

Illustrative average fare RWF 2,500 × proposed 8% = RWF 200 billed per completed trip. At 95% fee collection the cash receipt is RWF 190/trip. The RWF 2,500 is a modelling average, not a verified ASYV–Ntunga tariff.

Collect platform service fees from the driver/cooperative after completed trips, reconciled weekly. The passenger pays the driver after riding. Do not deduct money from uncompleted rides or treat driver fare receipts as Mova revenue.

Seek RWF 75,000/month for each institutional booking desk covering coordination, roster management and records within agreed hours. Base month three assumes two paying desks; none is currently committed.

Do not charge a new platform fee on top of an incumbent meter/platform obligation unless permitted and economically acceptable to drivers. If per-trip fees are rejected, test a partner-funded coordination contract; do not declare the same financial forecast still valid. Sponsorship/grants fund the experiment and are not recurring customer revenue.

## 4. How the service and money move

Passenger requests → driver accepts lawful fare/quote → driver arrives → passenger verifies plate and shares pickup code → driver starts and completes trip → passenger pays driver directly → both acknowledge receipt → Mova reconciles its service fee weekly.

The passenger fare is driver gross merchandise value (GMV), not Mova sales. If the illustrative average is RWF 2,500, the driver receives that fare after the trip and owes the proposed RWF 200 service fee only for a completed Mova-booked trip. Retained driver gross is RWF 2,300 before fuel, maintenance, existing fees and taxes; this is not net earnings.

The 8% fee, 95% collection and RWF 75,000 desk subscription are hypotheses. Obtain written operator/regulatory confirmation, check incumbent fees, and test willingness to pay. Double charging drivers may make the model unacceptable even if the spreadsheet balances.

Start without holding rider deposits or fare wallets. External cash/MoMo payment, provider charges and tax treatment must be confirmed with the licensed operating/payment partners. The current prototype only records acknowledgements; it does not verify money movement.

If per-trip fees are rejected, test institution-funded coordination or a cooperative software subscription as an alternative business, then rebuild its economics. Do not add speculative adverts, data sales or subsidies to hide a weak core model.

## 8. Unit economics and break-even

Base inputs: 20 drivers × 3 completed app trips/driver/day × 26 days = 1,560 trips/month. Average fare RWF 2,500 is illustrative and must be replaced with measured lawful fares, not the prototype card price.

GMV = RWF 3,900,000. Billed commission = 8% × GMV = RWF 312,000. At 95% collection, trip cash receipts = RWF 296,400, with RWF 15,600 uncollected. Add two desks × RWF 75,000 = RWF 150,000; total cash receipts = RWF 446,400.

Per completed trip: billed fee 200 × collection 95% = 190 cash receipts, minus variable provision 20 = RWF 170 contribution before fixed costs. Cancelled rides contribute no fee. A discounted or negotiated fare reduces this contribution; lower fare is not free to the platform.

Base cash break-even: CEILING((500,000 − 150,000) / 170) = 2,059 completed trips/month. That is 79.2/day or 3.96/driver/day. Google usage at this point still fits the modelled RWF 30,000 maps floor, so the fixed-cost formula is consistent for the base case.

Without institutional receipts, the workbook recalculates map/routing costs at every candidate volume. The first non-negative base cash result is 3,239 trips/month, about 6.23/driver/day. The simple fixed-cost shortcut would understate this because higher traffic makes Google usage chargeable.

Driver economics matter separately. Measure incremental trip income after all platform/meter fees, fuel/charging, maintenance, data and lost waiting time. More app trips do not automatically mean better take-home pay. No driver salary guarantee is assumed.

## 10. API, pricing and tax sensitivity

Google [S9]: the planning model uses 10,000 free monthly events for each relevant Essentials SKU, then USD 7/1,000 Dynamic Maps events and USD 5/1,000 Compute Routes events within the modelled volume tier. More advanced routing and other SKUs can cost more. Prices and aggregate billing-account usage must be checked before purchase.

Usage assumption: 80% request completion; four map loads and two route requests per attempted request; plus 1,000 non-trip map loads/month. These are budget assumptions, not measured logs. Browser reloads, previews, failures and visitors can materially increase events. Geocoding/other SKUs are not treated as unlimited; confirm event counts and include any overage before increasing traffic.

The FX input of RWF 1,600/USD is only a conservative planning conversion, not a claim about the spot rate. The model uses the larger of its API estimate and a RWF 30,000 monthly reserve. At pilot month three the event estimate stays within the listed free caps; at the expansion hypothesis the calculated maps/routes estimate is RWF 293,440/month.

Set API quotas, monitor billed usage, restrict the key and control unnecessary map reloads. Budget alerts are not spending caps. Do not switch to a public tile service expecting unlimited commercial usage; check its terms and service capacity.

RRA publishes VAT thresholds of RWF 20 million over 12 months or RWF 5 million in three consecutive months [S11]. Have an accountant confirm whether Mova is agent or principal, taxable platform receipts, EBM/invoicing and income-tax treatment. Fare GMV is not automatically the taxable platform turnover.

The model base VAT deduction is 0, not a declaration of exemption. If displayed service prices become VAT-inclusive with standard 18% applying [S12], set the workbook VAT deduction to 18%: commission and desk receipts available for operations fall. Supplier/import taxes may also change costs. No net-after-tax profit is asserted.

At a RWF 2,000 average fare and otherwise unchanged base terms, contribution is RWF 132/trip rather than RWF 170. At RWF 1,500 it is RWF 94. Fixed-budget break-even estimates rise to 2,652 and 3,724 trips/month respectively; API overage may raise them further. A ten-percentage-point drop in fee collection at the base fare removes RWF 20 contribution/trip.

## 12. Questions the team must answer

Why would someone use this instead of walking to the rank or calling a familiar driver?
Only if measured pickup reliability and saved coordination effort beat those options. Interviews and a manual pilot must show this; a map is not enough.

Does the problem matter in Rwanda?
Official household evidence indicates a transport-access gap, and ASYV has a real last-mile corridor. Neither proves demand for Mova specifically. We will not invent local survey results.

Why not launch nationally?
Small network density, human support and regulatory clarity matter more than nationwide downloads. One corridor keeps failure and spending contained.

Is bargaining definitely allowed?
No such claim is made. Applicable fare, meter and platform fee rules need confirmation. If bargaining is not allowed, remove it from commercial operation and retain compliant pricing and communication.

Why would drivers pay an 8% fee?
Only for incremental demand that improves take-home income after all existing charges. Eight percent is a test price, not a proven acceptable rate or regulatory approval.

How is the fee collected if passengers pay cash?
The driver keeps the fare at ride completion; Mova reconciles completed trips and invoices the cooperative/driver weekly. Modelled 95% collection allows 5% leakage. Monitor off-platform switching and unpaid balances.

Why would an institution pay RWF 75,000?
For useful coordination, a maintained roster and trip records, if a budget holder judges the savings worth the cost. A signed paid trial is the evidence; ASYV is not presented as an existing customer.

What prevents a rider paying and the driver not coming?
Mova collects no passenger fare in advance. The customer pays the driver after the completed trip. This avoids a platform-held fare deposit but cannot guarantee arrival, eliminate fraud or resolve every external cash/MoMo dispute.

What about people without smartphones?
Use an authorized partner desk or phone assistance first. This is partly a human service and must fit the coordinator's limited support hours; USSD is a later costed option.

Is the pilot profitable?
Not in the base month-three scenario: its cash operating shortfall is RWF 84,800 before founder replacement cost and income tax. The pilot is a paid validation experiment, not a claimed profitable launch.

What if we get no institutional buyers?
Accounting for higher Google usage at the required volume, base cash break-even rises from about four to about 6.2 completed app trips per driver/day. Do not count unsigned subscriptions.

What are the biggest hidden costs?
Human support, regulatory/insurance requirements, lawful data hosting, driver verification, fee leakage, map reloads and founder labour. The detailed budget exposes each rather than calling the platform free.

What about students and safety?
The first operating pilot serves consenting adults only. No unaccompanied-minor service, ambulance promise or guaranteed-safe label is offered. Institutional permission and operator insurance/document checks are required.

What makes Mova difficult to copy?
The software features are easy to copy. A trusted local roster, useful partner relationships, consistent pickup operations and repeat usage could become advantages; they do not exist just because a prototype works.

What is already built and what still needs work?
Built: two-sided demo, plate selection, communication, negotiation simulation, server-timed approach, pickup-code flow and pay-after acknowledgement. Still needed: verified accounts, production data/security controls, real opted-in driver GPS, operating approvals and institutional service processes.

What exactly are you asking for?
A maximum RWF 2.85 million conditional 90-day pilot envelope. Release only RWF 150,000 for initial discovery first; that is part of the setup budget, not extra. Release later tranches only after compliance/partner gates and weekly spending review.

## 13. Two-minute presentation script

Our project is Mova, a locally supported moto pickup service. We are starting with consenting adults around ASYV and Ntunga, not claiming a national launch. Official evidence makes last-mile access worth investigating, but we still need to prove local demand with interviews and observed pickups.

The customer chooses a plate, confirms a meeting point and communicates with the driver. After an agreed lawful quote, the driver arrives, a pickup code confirms the start, and payment happens after the trip. Our current demonstration uses an animated driver; it does not prove real-world travel time.

We propose an approved 8% service fee on completed app trips and RWF 75,000/month per institutional coordination desk. Both prices need willingness-to-pay tests and regulatory confirmation. We will not collect passenger deposits or charge cancelled rides.

We request a conditional RWF 2.85 million 90-day envelope, releasing RWF 150,000 for research first. Month three is still RWF 84,800 short on cash operations. With two paying institutions, cash break-even needs approximately four completed app trips per driver each operating day. The pilot must show demand, repeat use, better driver economics and reliable collection before expansion.

Our innovation is not simply putting bargaining on a map. It is proving whether a trusted, accountable pickup network with assisted access works better for this particular local community.
